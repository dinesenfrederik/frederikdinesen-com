You've spent months writing the songs and weekends recording them. Now they need mixing, and that brings up the question a lot of bands hesitate to ask: what does it actually cost? Many studios just say "contact us for a quote", and you have no idea whether to expect a few euros or half a month's rent per song.

As drummer and songwriter in the Danish metalcore band Rise Upon, I know the budget of an independent band from the inside. That's why my prices are public. In this guide I walk through what drives the price of a mix, the pricing models you'll come across, and what it costs to get a single, an EP and an album mixed.

In short, the price of a mix depends on the number of tracks, how much editing the tracks need, the number of revision rounds and whether mastering is included. Most mix engineers charge per song, with a discount for more songs. At FD Sound Labs a mix costs €335 per song for 1-2 songs and €295 per song for 3 or more, with 3 revision rounds included.

## How much does it cost to get a song mixed?

The price of getting a song mixed varies a lot: from very little on freelance platforms to considerably more with established producers who have big names on their résumé. The difference comes down to experience, genre knowledge, how much work is included and how in-demand the mix engineer is.

To give you a concrete reference point, here are my own prices at FD Sound Labs. All include 25% Danish VAT:

* **Single (1-2 songs):** €335 per song
* **EP or album (3 songs or more):** €295 per song

The EUR prices are guide prices based on my DKK prices. VAT-registered businesses in the EU outside Denmark are invoiced without Danish VAT under the reverse-charge rules.

**What matters most isn't the lowest number, but knowing exactly what you get for the money.** A cheap mix with no revisions can end up costing more if you have to get it redone somewhere else.

## What affects the price of a mix?

The price of a mix is set by how much time the song needs, and in heavy genres a lot of things take time. The main factors are:

* **Number of tracks:** A modern metal song often has 40 tracks or more, with several layers of rhythm guitar, bass split into sub and grit, drums, vocal doubles and synths. More tracks mean more work.
* **Editing:** Do the drums need tightening, the vocals tuning, or the guitars aligning? Editing can take as long as the mix itself.
* **Drums:** Programmed drums or rehearsal-room drums often need sample blending to get enough punch for a modern production.
* **Re-amping:** If you send clean DI tracks of guitar and bass, the tone can be shaped from scratch by re-amping (sending the DI signal through an amp or amp sim again). It sounds better, but it takes time.
* **Revision rounds:** How many times can the band ask for changes before the price goes up?
* **Deadline:** If it's urgent, some studios charge extra to move the project up the queue.

My price is fixed per song, so editing, sample blending and re-amping are included. Other studios price those separately, so always ask what's included.

## Per song, per hour or per day?

You'll usually come across three pricing models when getting music mixed: per song, hourly and day rates. Each has its pros and cons.

* **Per song:** The most common model for mixing. You know the price up front, and it's easy to budget a release. The downside is that you can't save money with a very simple song.
* **Hourly rate:** You pay for the time spent. It can be cheap for a simple song, but it's hard to budget, and a song with a lot of editing can add up.
* **Day rate:** Mostly used for recording in a studio, where the band books a full day. It's less common for mixing, because it's hard to know how many songs can be done in a day.

**For most bands a fixed price per song is the safest model**, because you know the amount before the work starts. That's also why I only work with fixed project prices.

## How much does it cost to mix an EP or an album?

Mixing an EP or an album usually costs the number of songs times the price per song, often with a volume discount as the project grows. With my prices it looks like this, including VAT:

* **Single, 1 song:** €335
* **Double single, 2 songs:** €670
* **EP, 5 songs:** €1,475
* **Album, 10 songs:** €2,950

If you also need to record vocals, guitar or bass in the studio, that comes on top. A full-day session in my studio in Aarhus, Denmark, is around €420. If you've recorded everything yourselves, you only pay for the mix.

> **Pro tip:** Plan which songs you're releasing first and get them mixed together. A combined project usually means a lower price per song, and the songs end up sounding like a whole instead of separate singles.

## Is mastering included in the price?

Mastering isn't always included in the price of a mix, and it's one of the things that surprises bands the most. Mixing and mastering are two different processes:

* **Mixing:** All the individual tracks are balanced, shaped with EQ and compression and placed in the stereo image, so they work as one song.
* **Mastering:** The finished stereo mix is polished so it gets the right loudness, translates across speakers and fits with the rest of the release.

Some studios do both, others only one. I'm a mix engineer and recommend having the final master done by a dedicated mastering engineer, so your release gets a fresh set of ears. By agreement, I can deliver a loud master of the mix for listening, sharing and testing the songs. **So set aside a separate budget for mastering when you plan your release.**

## What should be included in the price?

A mix is only as cheap as what's included. Use this checklist when you compare quotes from different mix engineers:

* **Revision rounds:** How many are included, and what do extra rounds cost?
* **Editing:** Are timing, tuning and noise clean-up included?
* **Drums:** Is sample blending or sample replacement included?
* **Delivery:** Do you get the final mix as 24-bit WAV, and can you get an instrumental or other versions?
* **Mastering:** Is it included, or do you need to find a mastering engineer yourself?
* **Payment:** Does the full amount have to be paid up front, or is it split?
* **Try before you buy:** Can you hear a section of your own song mixed before you pay?

With me, 3 revision rounds, editing, sample blending and re-amping are included, and payment is split 50/50, with a deposit at the start and the rest on approval. You can see the whole process on the [remote mixing](/en/remote-mixing) page.

## Is it worth mixing it yourself?

Mixing it yourself can be worth it if you have the time, a reasonably treated listening room and the motivation to learn the craft. Plenty of good productions start in a bedroom. But be honest about what you get out of it:

* **Time:** A heavy metal mix with lots of tracks can take days to get right, especially at first.
* **Listening room:** Without an acoustically treated room and proper monitors, it's hard to hear what's going on in the low end. That's where most DIY mixes go wrong, see also my guide to [fixing a muddy low end in a metal mix](/en/blog/fix-muddy-low-end-metal-mix).
* **Fresh ears:** When you wrote and recorded the song yourself, it's hard to hear it objectively.
* **AI tools:** Automatic mixing and mastering services are cheap and fast, but they don't make artistic decisions about how your song should sound.

**A good compromise is to record yourself and get a professional mix.** That way you spend your budget where it makes the biggest difference to the finished sound.

## How to get the most out of your budget

You get the most out of your budget when the mix engineer can spend the time mixing rather than cleaning up. That means:

1. **Record well from the start.** A clean take with freshly tuned instruments always beats a rescued recording.
2. **Prepare your tracks.** Export every track as 24-bit WAV from the same start point, and include the tempo and clean DI tracks.
3. **Send references.** 1-2 songs that sound like what you're aiming for save a lot of revision rounds.
4. **Collect the feedback.** Send the band's comments in one message instead of five messages from five members.
5. **Hear a test mix first.** If you want to hear how your own tracks sound in a professional mix before you pay anything, I offer a free, no-obligation test mix of a section of the song.

In the end, the price of a mix is about what you get for the money. Know the price up front, check what's included, and remember to set aside budget for mastering.
